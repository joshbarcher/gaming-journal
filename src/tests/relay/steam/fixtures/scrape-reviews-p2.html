Early Access Review
What an amazing survival game!

I have played a few of these with a friend, where the "playing with friends" really keeps the game alive. Usually the world looks nice, but the systems are lacking, combat is janky, and the good crafting keeps you coming back for more.

This game is in a league of its own in many ways. As far as I have seen, it's the most complete experience in these types of titles. The game is relatively balanced, has engaging combat, and the progression of the game continues to make sense and work until the end of the early-access content.

I really have to mention the world design. The map is astoundingly large and has been handcrafted almost at every level. There is very little copy-paste that I saw, and that is with a map that dwarfs others like Skyrim. There are 100s and 100s of buildings, dungeons, taverns, caves, fortresses, etc.Each of which are built with an interesting design and gameplay philosophy. This is usually where many survival games are paper thin, but the exploration is where this game really shines

The crafting is solid and keeps the progression moving. There are also more than a 100 types of craftable items that can be used for armor, gadgets, food, buildings, etc. Base-building is solid as well, with the best snapping and the least amount of jank I've seen in this type of game.

The game is also soloable, given a player that has decent skills in action-rpg games. Some of the bosses were a bit much, but overall the game requires you to learn the mechanics of the world and use them to your advantage. If you don't block, dodge, think your way through combat, then you will get stomped. Which is satisfying when you figure it out.

There is still a lot of room for improvement in EA:

- There needs to be more types of mobs for a world this huge. Even doubling the number of enemy types will not remove the samey feel in combat sometimes, Even though the combat was always fun (and challenging).
- The boss battles are fun, but a bit of a mess. Even though they are soloable, getting one shot by a boss is kind of annoying considering the size of the world and the run back. Having more bosses would be great, which I'm sure the developers will add.
- The item upgrades are well done, but there needs to be a longer progression curve. Getting a new weapon or armor felt great. But it happened so few times that I began to ignore this for much of the game.
- The game needs some optimization, as expected in EA.

The one annoyance I had with the game is the incessant valleys & mountains in the game. You will struggle to find how to traverse the world at many points and just run endlessly against walls because you're in a valley that stretches for miles and impedes your progress. It's a bit much and the game needs to double the number of exits for valleys and account for this with the gameplay. It's not fun jumping up walls that were never designed for such a thing because the effort required to get out of a valley is just not fun.

All in all though, this is a fantastic RPG-like experience in a massive world that will keep your attention for a long while. I heard the devs plan on drastically increasing the already enormous map O.O. So I'm looking forward to what they add. This game was a 9.5/10 for me, and something I didn't expect.

After 20+ hours I think I've seen enough. I'm really regretting having played this game, its just not fun.

Imagine those difficult pinches in other souls games where there is an enemy placed high up with a bow to harry you. Memorable sure. Now in this game, they have that guy in almost every fight.

Then you have 2 dogs and an axe wielder to fight, while the ranged guy is shooting you off screen. Okay, bad design, but could be fun to create momentary pressure. Then there is a tanky guy to fight with the group. He needs to be parried many times to slowly burn down his posture, then i can eviscerate him with a heavy attack. All this while being shot off screen. Then you notice there are 2 tanky guys in the group.

Somehow i kill the group. Even though multi-person fights basically are terrible in every souls game i have played. I then move to the next section and here is the same group in some other configuration. Then another group, then another.

The mob density and design is just plain busted.

I just can't figure out what the devs were going for here. Did they play their game? It's not fun. Souls games are hard, but fair. There is a satisfaction to the game. Almost a rhythm to accomplishing steps. This game is tedious and annoying, and the contrived difficulty is just aggravating.

I could go on with other problems about the floaty combat, unsatisfying (but accurate) parry, the forward movement on attacks, confusing mazelike world, the lack of navigation clues to new areas, relentless ganking enounters, etc. Long story short, i took a chance on the game. I wouldn't suggest this to any other players.

This is a difficult game to review. I have mixed feelings, even though I played the game 177 hours. I really love Bethesda and what they tried to do here, but there are many, many, many things that are not working in this game.

First, I did love a ton about the game. I am a huge fan of Skyrim and Fallout 4. I play Skyrim once a year, with it's amazing replayability. I had low expectations of the game and was generally surprised the further I played. Before I list the reasons I don't think the game worked, I believe the game deserves praise for several things:

- It made space cool! This needs to be an entire new genre in PC gaming. Now that a huge studio like Bethesda has shown what you can do with this type of game. This game took something I thought I was familiar with and intrigued me to the point that I might study more about the physical universe.
- The exploration in the game really hooked me. I surveyed hundreds of planets, just for the hell of it.
- Spaceships (on the surface) feel and play great. They are a stronghold to meet companions and a battleship to fight in.
- The expected elements of Bethesda games were there: lots of quests, branching dialogue, big world to explore, lots of items to pick up, good combat (pretty much).
- The main quest was actually good this time. Although, the ending really sucked. I'm disappointed that there wasn't an option at the end and you have to choose a certain path to get the credits (and the last few achievements). Generally speaking, I reject the choice.

Just because of the above, I am going to recommend the game. Even with the bad that I will list below, and there is A LOT of bad things in this game. But if you like Bethesda games, you might be disappointed, but you will have a fun time with the game.

So to the bad. The biggest things that bothered me while playing were the unfinished, broken, or otherwise weird game systems. I feel like it's obvious that the game was designed to work a certain way that included all components - exploration, outposts, spaceships, and quests/towns/stories. Then a late decision was made to redesign the first three systems so that they are unnecessary to complete the game.

- There is no reason to create outposts. Anything you could make or find can just be bought. And most things you buy in this way are cheap and easy to find. There is no incentive and the developers seem to have removed this from the game loop to progress beyond just progressing-for-progression sake. This should have been a requirement and help push your character forward. Even with outposts being simpler this time around (which I liked).
- There isn't a reason to explore planets. It isn't really required, even though I found it relaxing. Moreover, you get more XP from killing creatures on planets than anywhere else in the game. It's completely broken. You can turn in a request and get 100xp for your time, or I can kill a creature with a level of 30-50 and get the same xp. So I can run around and kill 10 creatures in 5 minutes while you spend 60 minutes doing 10 quests. Quest rewards need to be completed revamped, it makes no sense.
- I sided with the UC Sysdef and Collective against the Crimson Raiders. My reward, I can almost never find spaceships to fight. And when I do, I get 30 seconds of action, then nothing for 20-40 more minutes while I search around or try to find another mission at a terminal that leads to space fighting. This is broken and stupidly designed. I got to the leadership skill that requires 50 kills in a ship and I refuse to finish it. The space fights and exploration are just half-baked and replaced with fast-travel. It's entirely disappointing, considering that 1/5th of all talents in the game are for spaceships and space-fighting.
- At least 30% of all talents are just useless. SMH. Also, the XP curve is increasing so you never get more than around 20-30% of skills before you reach the end of the game.

Then the really bad.

- The game is shockingly buggy. Terribly so. I could barely talk to my love-interest at the end of the game since she bugged out. Terrible pathing of NPCs, floating NPCs, jank physics, broken quests left in my log at game end (6+ of them).
- NPCs look terrible in the game. Walking by your character all bug-eyed. I could barely even get my character to look not-terrible. And even he was bug-eyed during animations.
- The game has been taken over by woke/feminism, like many AAA games these days. I had entire quest-lines where 90% of speakers are edge-lord females. This is nothing like the real world or any world I want to live in. SMH. The game also seems entirely afraid to put a white male in interesting dialogue positions except for a few characters in constellation.
- I like that the game had representation of different races around the world. I don't appreciate the far-left anti-science views on gender or the romance being pushed with companions. I'm almost at the point now where I am no longer going to pay attention to romance in games, as it just becomes a place for far-left people to shill their morals on others, which I don't share.

All that being said, I do recommend the game. It kept me playing and exploring for almost 200 hours. It's not a bad game per se, but a deeply flawed experience. I think the best way to view it is to get in, get the fun you find in it, then finish the story and get out once the seams start showing in the game. For a moment, there will be a twinkle in your eye while bounding upon the surface of a moon somewhere being an explorer.

I wasn't on board for the settings, the shallow geopolitics conversations, or condescending story elements.
